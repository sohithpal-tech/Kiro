What is XAUUSD?
Decoding the symbol your bot trades
XAU
XAU is the international chemical symbol for Gold. It comes from the Latin word Aurum. This is the "base currency" — the thing being bought or sold.
USD
USD is the US Dollar. This is the "quote currency" — the currency you use to buy the gold. The price of XAUUSD tells you how many dollars one ounce of gold costs.
💡
When the XAUUSD price shows $2,400.00, it means: 1 ounce of Gold costs $2,400 US Dollars. Your bot is always watching this price, waiting for the right moment to buy or sell.

What is CFD Trading? (You Never Own Real Gold)
Understanding what you're actually doing when you "buy gold"

CFD stands for Contract for Difference. When your bot "buys gold," it is NOT purchasing physical gold bars. Instead, it's entering a contract with your broker that says: "Pay me the difference in price between when I enter and when I exit."

✅ What Actually Happens
  • Bot enters a BUY at $2,400.00
  • Gold rises to $2,430.00 (+$30)
  • Bot closes the trade
  • Broker pays your account the $30 difference × lot size
❌ What Does NOT Happen
  • No gold is purchased or stored
  • No gold is delivered to you
  • You are not a gold owner
  • No physical settlement occurs
⚠️
CFDs are leveraged products. This means profits AND losses are amplified. A small price move can produce a large dollar profit or loss depending on your lot size. This guide will show you exactly how to calculate this.

What is the Spread?
The hidden cost on every trade — paid the moment you open a position

When you want to buy Gold, your broker shows you TWO prices at the same time:

$2,400.30
ASK (Buy Price)
The price you pay to BUY Gold. Always the higher of the two prices. You enter a BUY trade at the ask.
$2,400.00
BID (Sell Price)
The price you receive when you SELL Gold. Always the lower price. A BUY trade is immediately "worth" the bid price.
📐 Spread Formula
Spread = Ask − Bid = $2,400.30 − $2,400.00 = $0.30 = 30 pips
This 30-pip gap is IMMEDIATELY deducted from your trade as a cost. You start every trade 30 pips in the negative.
⚠️
Gold Bot v7 checks the spread before every trade. If the spread exceeds 30 pips for TREND trades (or 20 pips for RANGE trades), the bot skips that cycle. A high spread during a news event or off-hours means the broker is charging more — your bot refuses to trade in those conditions.

What is Leverage?
How small capital controls large positions — the most important concept to understand

Leverage is like borrowing money from your broker to control a larger position. With 1:500 leverage, every $1 you put up controls $500 worth of gold.

📐 Leverage Formula
Position Size = Your Margin × Leverage
Example: $100 margin × 500 leverage = $50,000 of gold you control

🔢 Real Example: $10,000 account at 1:500 leverage

Your account balance:         $10,000
Your leverage:                1:500
Maximum gold you could control: $10,000 × 500 = $5,000,000

BUT — Gold Bot v7 only risks 1% per trade:
  Risk per trade:             $10,000 × 1% = $100
  Actual position opened:     ~0.05 lots (~$12,000 of gold)
  Margin used:                ~$24 (tiny fraction of your account)
⚠️
Leverage is a double-edged sword. If gold moves 1% against you on a 1:500 leveraged position at maximum size, you would lose 500% of your margin. That's why your bot NEVER uses maximum leverage. It calculates exactly how many lots to trade so that even if the worst happens (SL gets hit), you only lose 1% of your account — not your entire balance.
ℹ️
Brokers like Exness offer up to 1:2000 leverage on Gold. Your bot uses this leverage as a convenience to open small positions without tying up large amounts of margin — NOT to maximise exposure. The risk control comes from lot sizing, not from limiting leverage directly.

💰 Profit & Loss Calculation
How every dollar is made or lost. Worked examples using real Gold prices and bot lot sizes.
📐 The Master P&L Formula (for Gold on MT5)
P&L (USD) = (Exit Price − Entry Price) × Lots × 100
The 100 comes from: 1 lot = 100 oz, pip = $0.01, so pip value = 100 × $0.01 = $1 per lot. For a BUY: positive = profit. For a SELL: positive means price went down (also profit).
ℹ️
The formula your bot uses in code: (fill_price - entry_price) * lots * 100 for a BUY trade. For a SELL trade it's (entry_price - fill_price) * lots * 100. The 100 factor combines: 100 oz per lot × $0.01 pip value = $1 effective pip value per lot.

Commission vs Spread — What You Actually Pay
Two different cost structures depending on your broker
Spread-Based Broker
  • The spread (bid/ask gap) IS the cost
  • No separate commission charged
  • Typical Gold spread: 15–35 pips normally
  • Common with "standard" or "cent" accounts
  • Cost is hidden inside the price itself

  • Common with "Pro" or "ECN" accounts

The most important concepts in risk management. What 1R, 1.5R, 2R, 3R means — and how your bot uses them.
SL
What is a Stop Loss (SL)?
Your automatic safety net — closes the trade before losses become catastrophic

A Stop Loss is a price level you set in advance. If the market reaches that price against your position, your trade is automatically closed. It's like saying "if this goes wrong by this much, get me out automatically."

🔴 Example: BUY trade with Stop Loss

Entry (BUY):      $2,400.00
Stop Loss:        $2,382.00  ← automatic close if price drops here
                 ─────────────────────────────────────
SL Distance:      $18.00 (1,800 pips / 18 "points" in Gold price)

If SL is hit, loss = (2400.00 - 2382.00) × lots × 100
With 0.05 lots:   18.00 × 0.05 × 100 = $90.00 maximum loss
⚠️
NEVER trade without a Stop Loss. Gold can move $50+ in minutes during news events. Without a SL, a single bad trade can wipe out weeks of profits. Gold Bot v7 ALWAYS sets a SL before entering any position. There are no exceptions.

How the Bot Calculates Stop Loss (ATR Method)

Your bot doesn't pick random SL levels. It uses the ATR (Average True Range) — a measure of how much Gold typically moves per candle — multiplied by 1.5 to give the trade room to breathe without being too risky.

📐 Bot's Stop Loss Formula
SL (BUY) = Entry Price − (ATR × 1.5)
SL (SELL) = Entry Price + (ATR × 1.5)
ATR is calculated over 14 periods on the 15-minute chart. The ×1.5 multiplier gives the trade enough room so normal volatility doesn't accidentally hit your stop.

🔢 Worked Example: ATR = $12.00

Current Gold price:    $2,400.00
ATR (14-period 15M):   $12.00 (Gold moves avg $12 per 15min candle)
SL multiplier:         1.5

SL Distance = $12.00 × 1.5 = $18.00
SL Price    = $2,400.00 − $18.00 = $2,382.00

This means: Gold needs to drop $18 from your entry before the bot accepts
it has gone wrong and exits. Normal candle wobble of $12 won't touch your SL.

What is a Take Profit (TP)?
Your profit target — locks in gains when price reaches your goal

A Take Profit is the opposite of a Stop Loss. It's a price level where your trade automatically closes to lock in profit. Once the price hits your TP level, the trade closes immediately — no hesitation, no second-guessing.

🟢 Example: BUY trade with Take Profit

Entry (BUY):      $2,400.00
Take Profit:      $2,454.00  ← trade closes automatically here
Stop Loss:        $2,382.00
                 ─────────────────────────────────────
Risk:             $18.00 (SL distance)
Reward:           $54.00 (TP distance)
Risk:Reward:      1:3 (risking $18 to make $54)

Your bot sets a TP reference at 3× the SL distance (ATR × 3.0 = ATR × 1.5 × 2). However — the bot doesn't always close at this TP. It uses a tiered exit instead (30% + 30% + trail). The TP reference is a guide, not a hard close.

📐 Bot's Take Profit Reference Formula
TP Ref (BUY) = Entry Price + (ATR × 3.0)
This is the theoretical 3R target. In practice, the bot partially closes at 1R and 2R, then trails the rest.

What Does "R" Mean? (The Most Important Concept)
R is the universal language of trading risk management

R = Risk. Specifically, 1R equals the dollar amount you risked on a trade (the distance from entry to your Stop Loss, in dollar terms). Every trade result can be expressed as a multiple of this initial risk.

📐 Definition of 1R
1R = |Entry Price − Stop Loss Price| × Lots × 100
Example: Entry $2,400, SL $2,382, 0.05 lots → 1R = 18 × 0.05 × 100 = $90

Why Does 1.25R Appear in the Trade Log?
Understanding blended R — the weighted average of a tiered exit

The R number in your trades_log.csv is a blended R — the weighted average of all the partial closes combined. Because the bot exits in 3 tiers, the blended R is always somewhere between the first tier target and the trailing stop exit.

🔢 How Blended R is Calculated

Position: 0.10 lots total
Entry: $2,400 | SL: $2,382 | 1R distance = 18 pips

TIER 1: Close 30% at +1R
  Weight: 30%
  R earned: 1.0R
  Contribution: 0.30 × 1.0 = 0.30R

TIER 2: Close 30% at +2R
  Weight: 30%  
  R earned: 2.0R
  Contribution: 0.30 × 2.0 = 0.60R

TIER 3: Trail 40% — closes at +1.5R (e.g., trailing stop captured)
  Weight: 40%
  R earned: 1.5R
  Contribution: 0.40 × 1.5 = 0.60R

BLENDED R = 0.30 + 0.60 + 0.60 = 1.50R

If TIER 3 trailed and closed early at +1.0R:
  0.40 × 1.0 = 0.40R
  Blended R = 0.30 + 0.60 + 0.40 = 1.30R

If TIER 3 ran to full 3R:
  0.40 × 3.0 = 1.20R
  Blended R = 0.30 + 0.60 + 1.20 = 2.10R
💡
A blended R of 1.25R–1.5R is typical for trades where the trailing stop on the final 40% is captured somewhere between 1R and 2R. This is the bot's most common win scenario — not a full home-run, but consistently positive results that compound over many trades.

🔢 Lot Sizing — How the Bot Calculates Position Size
Every trade is sized to risk exactly 1% of your account. Here's the exact formula — with worked examples.
💡
Position sizing is the most important risk management tool you have. The goal is simple: no matter how large or small your account, no single trade should ever risk more than 1% of your equity. This is what separates professional traders from gamblers.
📐
The Complete Lot Size Formula
Used by the bot in NORMAL mode (1% risk)
📐 Step 1: Calculate Risk Amount
Risk Amount = Account Equity × Risk %
NORMAL mode = 1.0% → $10,000 × 0.01 = $100 maximum risk per trade
📐 Step 2: Calculate SL Distance in Points
SL Distance = |Entry Price − SL Price|
Example: Entry $2,400, SL $2,382 → SL Distance = $18.00 = 1,800 pips (18 "price points")
📐 Step 3: Calculate Lot Size
Lot Size = Risk Amount ÷ (SL Distance × $1)
The $1 per pip per lot is the pip value for Gold. SL Distance here is in price points ($), so we use the raw dollar distance.

🔢 Full Worked Example

Account Equity:    $10,000
Risk Percentage:   1.0% (NORMAL mode)
Risk Amount:       $10,000 × 0.01 = $100

Entry Price:       $2,400.00
ATR (14-period):   $12.00
SL = Entry − ATR×1.5 = $2,400 − $18 = $2,382.00
SL Distance:       $18.00

Pip Value per lot: $1.00 (for Gold XAUUSD)

Raw Lot Size = $100 ÷ ($18.00 × $1.00)
             = $100 ÷ $18.00
             = 5.555... lots

Round to broker minimum step (0.01):
             → 5.55 lots (maximum)

BUT: Bot also caps at volume_max (broker limit)
     and enforces volume_min (0.01 lots minimum)

Final rounded lot size → 0.05 lots (see below)
⚠️
Wait — the formula gave us 5.55 lots, but the bot trades 0.05 lots?! This is because the formula above is in raw units where SL distance = $18. But in the actual MT5 API, lot sizing uses the tick value system. The formula effectively becomes: lot = risk_amount ÷ (sl_pips × tick_value / tick_size) where tick_value = 0.01 and tick_size = 0.01 for Gold. This gives 100× smaller number — so 5.55 ÷ 100 = 0.0555 → rounds to 0.05 lots. See the code equivalent below.

🐍 The Actual Bot Code Logic (simplified)

equity     = mt5.account_info().equity          # e.g. $10,000
risk_pct   = get_mode("risk_pct")               # 0.01 (1%)
risk_amt   = equity * risk_pct                  # $100

sl_dist    = abs(entry_price - sl_price)        # $18.00
tick_val   = mt5.symbol_info("XAUUSD").trade_tick_value   # 0.01
tick_sz    = mt5.symbol_info("XAUUSD").trade_tick_size    # 0.01

# pip_value_per_lot = tick_val / tick_sz = 0.01 / 0.01 = 1.0  ← $1/pip/lot
pip_value  = tick_val / tick_sz

raw_lots   = risk_amt / (sl_dist * pip_value)  # 100 / (18 * 1.0) = 5.555
# But sl_dist is in price units ($18), not in ticks
# So for Gold where 1 tick = $0.01:
# sl_dist in ticks = 18 / 0.01 = 1800 ticks
# raw_lots = 100 / (1800 * 0.01) = 100 / 18 = 5.55  ← same answer

# Round to nearest 0.01
final_lots = round(raw_lots / vol_step) * vol_step    # → 0.05

Why the Bot Uses EQUITY, Not BALANCE
A critical distinction that protects you during losing streaks
Balance
Your starting cash in the account. Does NOT update while trades are open. If you started with $10,000 and have 2 open trades losing $150 total, your balance still shows $10,000.
Equity
Balance + floating P&L. Updates in real time. If you started with $10,000 and have −$150 floating, equity = $9,850. This is your true current wealth.

🔢 Why This Matters During a Losing Streak

Starting balance:   $10,000

Trade 1: LOSS −$100. Balance=$10,000. Equity=$9,900.
Trade 2 sizing (using EQUITY):
  Risk = $9,900 × 1% = $99 (slightly less than $100)

Trade 2: LOSS −$99. Balance=$10,000. Equity=$9,801.
Trade 3 sizing (using EQUITY):
  Risk = $9,801 × 1% = $98.01 (keeps shrinking)

→ Your losses SHRINK automatically during a bad run
→ You would need 69 consecutive 1% losses to lose half your account
→ Using BALANCE instead would keep risking $100 regardless
✅
This is called "fixed fractional position sizing" — one of the most important concepts in professional trading. The bot does this automatically. During winning streaks, lot sizes grow. During losing streaks, they shrink. This creates a natural protection against blowing your account.

Section D — The 4 Strategy Modules

M1
MODULE 01 — TREND (SMC + Order Block + OTE Entry)
Used in TREND_BULL and TREND_BEAR regimes — aims for 3R target

The TREND module uses Smart Money Concepts (SMC) to find high-probability entries in the direction of the trend. It requires multiple confirmations before entering — each one filters out false signals.

  • 4H Bias Check — The 4-hour chart must agree with the 1H trend direction. If 1H says BULL but 4H is bearish, skip. Multi-timeframe alignment required.
  • Session Check — Must be within the 06:00–16:00 UTC trading window. Optionally enforces Silver Bullet windows (10–11 AM and 2–3 PM London time) for highest-quality entries.
  • Sweep Candle Detection — Looks for a candle that "swept" liquidity. A bullish sweep: candle wick goes below a recent low (hunting stop-losses), then closes above it. Body must be ≥38% of total candle range. This signals institutional buying after trapping retail shorts.
  • MSS (Market Structure Shift) Candle — After the sweep, waits for a candle that breaks the previous structure. For BUY: close must break above the 10-bar high. Body ≥40% of range. This confirms the reversal is real.
  • FVG / Order Block / OTE Entry — Enters at a Fair Value Gap (imbalance) or Order Block (last opposing candle before the move) or Fibonacci 61.8% OTE retracement zone. These are price levels where smart money is likely to buy more.
  • RSI Guard — For BUY: RSI must be below 75 (not overbought). For SELL: RSI must be above 25 (not oversold). Prevents entering at price extremes.
  • Signal Generated! — All checks passed. Proceeds to ML filter and lot sizing.
💡
SMC (Smart Money Concepts) is a trading framework based on understanding how large institutional traders (banks, hedge funds) move price. Instead of following retail indicators, SMC focuses on: liquidity hunts (stop-loss sweeps), fair value gaps (imbalances), and order blocks (supply/demand zones). Your bot uses these concepts to trade WITH the institutions, not against them.
M2
MODULE 02 — RANGE (Range Boundary SMC)
Used in RANGE regime — buys at bottom, sells at top
  • Range Confirmed — Verifies Gold has been trading within a 60–200 pip band for at least 8 candles. The boundaries are identified as the range high (resistance) and range low (support).
  • Boundary Touch — Price must currently be at (within 10% of) the range high or range low. Not in the middle.
  • Rejection Candle — The candle at the boundary must show rejection: a long wick into the boundary zone and a close back away from it. Candle body ≥40% of range. This shows price is being pushed back from the boundary.
  • RSI Divergence — Optional confirmation: price makes new low at range bottom but RSI makes higher low = hidden bullish divergence. Increases signal quality but not always required.
  • Bollinger Band Touch — Price touching the outer Bollinger Band (2.5 standard deviations) at the range boundary provides additional confirmation of mean-reversion potential.
ℹ️
Range trades use a tighter target than trend trades — typically 1.5R–2R rather than 3R — because range-bound Gold often reverses before reaching extended targets. The SL is still ATR×1.5, but the bot expects to exit at the opposite boundary of the range.
M3
MODULE 03 — VOLATILE SQUEEZE (Bollinger Squeeze Breakout)
Used in VOLATILE_SQUEEZE regime — trades the expansion after compression
  • ATR Compression Confirmed — Current ATR must be at its lowest level in the last 20 periods. Gold has been unusually quiet.
  • Bollinger Band Squeeze — BB width must be at a 252-bar minimum (1 year of daily data = approximately at all-time narrow). This extreme compression precedes large moves.
  • No Nearby News — No high-impact news within the next 2 hours. A squeeze broken by news is unpredictable — the bot avoids these.
  • Breakout Candle — A candle closes outside the Bollinger Bands with a body ≥45% of its range. This is the signal that volatility expansion has begun.
  • 4H Alignment — The breakout direction must align with the 4H bias. A downside breakout in an uptrending 4H chart would be filtered out.
  • Entry — Enters immediately in the breakout direction. SL is ATR×1.5 (remember: ATR is at a low right now, so the SL is actually quite tight — which means the lot size is larger than usual!).
M4
MODULE 04 — POST-NEWS (ICT News Entry)
Used in VOLATILE_NEWS regime — enters after news spike settles
  • Spike Detection — An ATR spike must exist in the last 6 candles: spike ATR > 1.5× the average ATR. A big candle appeared after a news event.
  • 60-Minute Wait — At least 60 minutes must have passed since the spike. This allows the initial chaos to settle and a more structured move to form.
  • Consolidation Tight — In the 60 minutes since the spike, price must NOT have retraced more than 35% of the spike's range. If it did, the spike has reversed — no trade.
  • Sweep in Spike Direction — A small liquidity sweep in the direction of the spike (a micro stop-hunt before continuation). This is the classic ICT "judas swing" concept.
  • FVG Confirmation — A Fair Value Gap must be present from the spike candle. Price re-entering this gap is the entry signal.
  • Entry at 1.5R Target — Post-news trades use TP = ATR×1.5×1.5 instead of ×3.0. The target is smaller but the probability is higher in this scenario.
💡
The post-news module is triggered when events like US CPI, Federal Reserve decisions, or Non-Farm Payrolls create a sudden $15–30 spike in Gold. Rather than chasing the move blindly, the bot waits for a specific setup that shows the market has "decided" direction. This is far safer than trading during the initial volatility.

Win Rate

Win Rate = COUNT(result="WIN") ÷ COUNT(result not blank)

In Excel/Sheets:
=COUNTIF(S2:S100,"WIN")/COUNTA(S2:S100)

Total P&L

Total P&L = SUM(pnl_usd) for all completed trades
Example: 15 wins averaging $85 each
         7 losses averaging $68 each
Total = (15 × $85) − (7 × $68) = $1,275 − $476 = $799

In Excel/Sheets:
=SUMIF(S2:S100,"<>",T2:T100)

Section B — What is the Machine Learning System?

🧠
ML Phase 1: Why 200 Trades First?
The bot needs data before it can learn

The ML system is a personal AI that learns from your specific trading history. It is NOT a generic trading algorithm. It learns: "given these exact market conditions, does MY bot, at MY broker, on MY account, tend to win or lose?"

⚠️
Before 200 closed trades: ML is completely inactive. The bot runs in "placeholder mode" — every signal that passes the module checks is automatically approved. The ML filter is disabled. You will see "ML: Placeholder mode (X/200 trades)" in the Telegram messages.

💡 Why 200 Trades?

Imagine trying to learn if a coin is fair by flipping it 10 times.
You might get 8 heads — but that doesn't mean it's biased.
You need at least 100–200 flips to be confident.

Trading signals are similar. With 30 trades, you can't distinguish:
  "this setup genuinely has edge" from "got lucky for 3 weeks"

The ML models need to see enough examples of BOTH wins AND losses
in MULTIPLE market regimes, sessions, and conditions to learn
reliable patterns. 200 trades is the minimum for statistical significance.

An ML model trained on less than this is like a weather forecast
based on 3 days of observations. The noise overwhelms the signal.
🤖
The 3 ML Models — What They Are and How They Vote
Ensemble voting: 3 models, 1 decision
MODEL 1 — 40% WEIGHT
XGBoost
Gradient Boosting Decision Tree. The strongest model in practice. Builds hundreds of small decision trees, each one learning from the mistakes of the previous. Excellent at finding non-linear patterns in structured data.
Weight: 40%
MODEL 2 — 30% WEIGHT
Random Forest
An ensemble of decision trees, each trained on a random subset of your data. Less prone to overfitting than XGBoost alone. Acts as a "second opinion" that catches patterns the XGBoost model might overfit.
Weight: 30%
MODEL 3 — 30% WEIGHT
Logistic Regression
Simple statistical model. Finds linear relationships between features and win/loss outcomes. The "sanity check" — if LR strongly disagrees with XGBoost and RF, something unusual is happening.
Weight: 30%
📐 Ensemble Vote Formula
Score = (XGB × 0.40) + (RF × 0.30) + (LR × 0.30)
Each model outputs a probability from 0.0 to 1.0 (probability that this trade will be a WIN). The weighted average must exceed the mode threshold to allow the trade.

How the models keep improving over time
  • Collect all completed trades — Reads every row in trades_log.csv where "result" is not blank.

✅
Walk-forward validation is important. If you randomly shuffled the data and tested on random 20%, you'd be testing the model on data that happened BEFORE some of the training data — which is data leakage and gives falsely optimistic results. Walk-forward validation always tests on the FUTURE relative to training data, mimicking real-world conditions.

LEVEL 2 Understanding the Indicators Week 3–4
ℹ️
Your bot uses 5 key indicators to understand market conditions. You don't need to be a technical analysis expert — you just need to understand what each indicator is measuring and why the bot uses it.
  • EMA (Exponential Moving Average) — An average of recent prices that gives more weight to recent candles. EMA50 is the "near-term trend". EMA200 is the "long-term trend". When EMA50 crosses above EMA200 = bullish. Add these to your MT5 chart and watch them for a few days.
  • ADX (Average Directional Index) — Measures the STRENGTH of a trend, NOT its direction. ADX > 28 = strong trend. ADX < 20 = no trend (ranging market). Important: ADX can be 40 in both an uptrend AND a downtrend. It tells you HOW trending, not WHICH direction.
  • RSI (Relative Strength Index) — Measures momentum on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 = overbought (may reverse down). Below 30 = oversold (may reverse up). Your bot uses RSI as a guard — refuses to buy when RSI > 75 or sell when RSI < 25.
  • ATR (Average True Range) — The average size of candles over 14 periods. If ATR = $15 on the 15M chart, that means Gold moves an average of $15 per 15-minute candle. High ATR = volatile = wider SL needed = smaller lot size. Add ATR to your chart and watch it spike during news events.
  • Bollinger Bands — A moving average surrounded by 2 standard deviation bands. When bands are wide = high volatility. When bands are narrow (squeeze) = low volatility about to break out. The squeeze module specifically watches for the narrowest Bollinger Bands in a year.
LEVEL 3 SMC Concepts (Your Bot's Entry Logic) Week 4–5
ℹ️
Smart Money Concepts (SMC) are the foundational ideas behind how your bot finds entry signals. Understanding these will help you read the goldbot.log and understand WHY the bot entered a specific trade at a specific price.
  • Liquidity Sweep — Big banks/institutions know where retail traders place their stop-losses (just below obvious lows, just above obvious highs). Before reversing, price often "sweeps" through these stop-loss clusters to collect liquidity. After the sweep, the real move begins. Your bot looks for sweep candles as the first signal in Module 01.
  • MSS (Market Structure Shift) — The first candle to close above the previous swing high (in a bullish reversal) after a sweep. This is the confirmation that the short-term trend has changed. Body must be ≥40% of candle range — showing conviction, not just a wick.
  • FVG (Fair Value Gap) — When price moves so fast that it creates a 3-candle imbalance: candle 1 high < candle 3 low (for bullish FVG). The gap in the middle was skipped — institutions often return to fill this "gap" before continuing. Your bot uses FVG levels as entry triggers.
  • Order Block (OB) — The last bearish candle before a strong bullish move (or last bullish candle before a strong bearish move). Institutions placed large orders here. When price returns to this zone, they often add more. Your bot enters at or near Order Blocks.
  • OTE (Optimal Trade Entry) — Fibonacci 61.8% — After a sweep and structure shift, waiting for price to retrace to the 61.8% Fibonacci level before entering. This is the "optimal" entry point — you're buying after a dip, not chasing the initial move. The bot uses 61.8–78.6% as the OTE zone.

📌 Concept 13 — Liquidity Sweeps and Stop Hunts

The Problem Retail Traders Create

Most retail traders place stop-losses just below obvious swing lows (for longs) or above swing highs (for shorts). This creates clusters of stop-loss orders — called "liquidity pools" — at predictable price levels.

What Smart Money Does

Institutional traders need massive order fills. They push price INTO these stop-loss clusters, triggering thousands of orders at once. This provides them liquidity to fill their own positions. The spike through + close back inside = a liquidity sweep.

🔗 How Your Bot Detects This

detect_liquidity_sweep() looks for on 15M Gold:
  1. Previous swing low identified (where retail stops cluster)
  2. Candle LOW dips BELOW that previous swing low  ← the sweep / "stop hunt"
  3. Candle CLOSE returns ABOVE that previous swing low ← smart money reversed
  4. Candle body >= 38% of range  ← genuine conviction, not random wick
All 4 = sweep confirmed → proceed to MSS check
